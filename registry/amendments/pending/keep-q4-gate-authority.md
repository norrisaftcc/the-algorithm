## The rule

**The signer is whoever bears the consequence. Scope gates by blast radius, not by rank.**

This is not new. It is belief 7 read in reverse:

> A gate is real because a human pays to open it.

If the signer does not pay, the signature is not a gate — it is a keystroke. If someone else pays, that someone else is the signer. Q4 reads as an open question only while authority is imagined as a property of seniority; as a property of exposure it resolves.

## What follows

**1. No countersignature. Two gates instead.**

A senior seat does not countersign a junior seat's freeze. Two signatures on one gate means neither signer fully pays, and the junior learns that someone catches their mistakes — which removes exactly the cost the gate exists to impose.

Where consequences genuinely cross a boundary, that is **two gates**, not one gate with two signers:

| Consequence lands… | Signer |
|---|---|
| Inside the junior seat's sandbox — its own repository, its own fan-outs, its own spend | the junior seat, alone |
| Across the boundary — shared trunk, another party's work, anything public, real users, institutional spend | the senior seat, separately |

A seat never gains authority over consequences it does not bear. This is not a restriction on the junior gate; it is what makes the junior gate real.

**2. Gate color is already determined, where a clearance ladder exists.**

SPECTRUM §6 already says a gate opens only to a live human at the gate's color or above, and that amendment, adjudication, and spawn are GREEN. Any ladder that assigns a seat a color therefore already answers "may this seat open this gate" — by arithmetic, not by ruling. Where a downstream context tops out below GREEN, seats in it hold no spawn authority, and material handing them a fan-out to authorize is a violation rather than a design question.

This is offered as a **finding about existing canon**, not an amendment: §5 and §6 already entail it. What is missing is that the two sections are not read against each other, so the entailment goes unnoticed at exactly the moment someone needs it.

**3. The freeze log is evidence. It is not a score.**

K6 already names why: *every mechanism installed creates the incentive to perform it.* Score the count of freezes and the system manufactures ritual freezes — the liturgy-performance failure, arriving through the front door.

What the log supports is judgment assessment, which reads the same artifact without counting it: did the seat negotiate before freezing; did it refuse to freeze something underspecified; did it reopen on failure rather than patch; did it decline to freeze what it had not read.

## Two items Q4 does not ask, and should

**Record status.** Wherever a freeze log is consulted in evaluating a person, it acquires whatever record obligations that jurisdiction attaches to evaluation records — retention, disclosure, correction. "Evidence, not score" softens this and does not remove it. The register should decide this deliberately rather than inherit it.

**Revocation.** If a seat freezes something it should not have, is its authority withdrawn? The answer consistent with belief 7 is **no** — the consequence lands and the record holds it, because a gate one can be rescued from is not one that was paid for. That reasoning is clean in a way real people are not, and where the seat is held by a person with a duty of care owed to them, the cost of being right about it is borne by someone who did not choose the rule. Flagged as a live tension, not resolved.

## Provenance

Produced while ruling a downstream acceptance gate that had to scope its signer narrowly because Q4 was open, and that recorded the gap rather than closing it silently. The downstream ruling is the worked example; this filing is the general form with the local detail removed.

The downstream context is a two-course sequence whose clearance ladder tops out below GREEN in the first course and reaches GREEN at exit of the second. That shape is what made §2 visible: the junior context could not hold a spawn gate no matter who was asked, and no one had noticed because the two governing documents were never read together.
